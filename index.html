How it works

A viewing shows a house at one moment, usually on a weekday afternoon. What the street is like on a Friday night, whether the neighbours' parties go on until three in the morning, whether the quiet road becomes a shortcut at rush hour — none of that is visible during a twenty-minute visit. AreaWatch sends an investigator to watch the area for you before you commit.

You give us the address and tell us what worries you: noise, parking, a particular neighbour, anti-social behaviour, traffic. The investigator plans observation over seven to ten days, at different times — mornings, evenings, nights, weekdays and the weekend — so that the report covers the life of the street, not a single moment.

During the observation the investigator notes what happens around the property: noise and its sources, parking and traffic, groups gathering, deliveries, anything unusual at the neighbouring houses. Observation is done discreetly from public places, without entering private property and without contacting the neighbours.

Alongside the observation, the investigator checks publicly available information about the area that is relevant to your concerns. Everything is recorded with dates and times.

At the end you receive a written report: what was observed, when, and what it means for the concerns you raised. You can discuss the report with the investigator by phone before you decide.

The report is meant to help you decide before you exchange contracts, when walking away is still simple. It is a record of observations, not a guarantee about the future or about the people who live nearby.

AreaWatch is for private buyers anywhere in England, Scotland and Wales. The price is a fixed fee per property, agreed before the work begins.

Before the work starts, we agree the scope with you in a short call: which concerns matter most, which times of day are most important and how quickly you need the report. If you are under time pressure because of a mortgage offer or an auction date, the observation can be planned so that the report arrives in time for your decision.

The investigator keeps a log for every session, with the date, the start and end time and what was observed. If something of interest happens repeatedly — a car parked across a driveway every evening, loud music at weekends — the report says how often it happened during the observation period, so you can judge how typical it is.

You can order a second, shorter check after moving in, for example if a problem appears that you want documented. Prices for follow-up checks are agreed in advance.

AreaWatch does not replace a survey, local authority searches or legal advice from your conveyancer. All observation is carried out lawfully and from public places.
